# Topicora Editorial Guide

## Editorial promise

Topicora makes useful, cross-disciplinary ideas understandable without flattening their uncertainty. Write for a curious general reader: informed, direct, generous, and skeptical of hype. A reader should finish knowing what is supported, what is inference, what remains unknown, and what action is reasonable.

## Voice and structure

- Lead with the reader's question or practical tension, not a long scene-setter.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs. Explain unavoidable technical terms on first use.
- Use short paragraphs, informative headings, and examples that do real explanatory work.
- Distinguish evidence from interpretation. Attribute claims close to where they appear.
- Avoid manufactured certainty, clickbait, unsupported superlatives, and generic AI phrasing.
- End with a useful synthesis or next step rather than repeating the introduction.

A standard article should contain:

1. A descriptive title and an excerpt that explains the value of reading.
2. A direct introduction that frames the question.
3. Two or more well-labeled sections supported by sources or transparent reasoning.
4. Caveats, counterevidence, or limitations where relevant.
5. A concise conclusion and source links.

## Research and sourcing

Prefer primary sources: original research, official datasets, standards, filings, court records, direct transcripts, and product documentation. Use reputable secondary sources for context, not as a substitute for a checkable original claim. Link to the most direct stable source and record access dates when content is likely to change.

Verify names, dates, units, quotations, statistics, and causal language. A correlation is not a cause. One study is not a consensus. Clearly identify estimates, modeled outcomes, preprints, small samples, conflicts of interest, and contested findings.

Never invent a source, quotation, statistic, personal experience, or interview. If a fact cannot be verified, remove it or label the uncertainty plainly.

## Disclosure and independence

Choose the article disclosure that matches the content:

- `none`: no special relationship or framing.
- `opinion`: analysis substantially reflects the author's judgment.
- `financial`: the author or publication has a relevant financial interest.
- `affiliate`: Topicora may earn from linked purchases.
- `sponsored`: an outside party funded or influenced the assignment; explain the boundary.
- `ai_assisted`: generative AI materially assisted research, drafting, translation, or imagery.

The disclosure note should name the material fact in plain language. A disclosure does not excuse weak sourcing or undisclosed influence. Editorial conclusions remain independent of advertisers, affiliates, and sponsors.

## AI assistance

Humans remain accountable for every published sentence. AI may help brainstorm, transcribe, translate, outline, or suggest edits, but an editor must independently verify all claims, citations, quotations, and calculations. Do not upload confidential sources, unreleased financial data, credentials, subscriber information, or personal contact messages to an AI service.

Disclose material AI assistance. AI-generated or substantially edited images must be labeled in their caption unless their synthetic nature is obvious and the editorial policy explicitly permits otherwise. Do not use synthetic media to depict a real event as documentary evidence.

## High-stakes subjects

Financial stories must distinguish education from personalized advice, show relevant assumptions, and include the site disclaimer. Never promise returns. Medical and health stories should rely on current authoritative guidance, distinguish population evidence from individual care, and tell readers to consult a qualified professional where appropriate. Legal, safety, and crisis information requires specialist review before publication.

## Images and accessibility

Use images the publication owns, licenses, or may lawfully reuse. Record credit and license information outside the alt field. Write concise alt text that communicates the image's purpose in context; use an empty alt only for genuinely decorative images. Do not begin alt text with “image of.” Captions should add context, attribution, or disclosure rather than repeat the alt text.

Compress raster files and choose a sensible crop. Do not upload SVG, confidential images, screenshots containing personal data, or files larger than the configured 5 MiB limit.

## SEO without distortion

- Use the language a reader would search, but make the title accurate in isolation.
- Keep SEO titles at 70 characters or fewer and descriptions at 170 or fewer.
- Choose one stable lowercase hyphenated slug. Avoid changing it after publication; create an internal redirect when change is unavoidable.
- Set a canonical URL only when another URL should be treated as the authoritative version.
- Never add keywords that the article does not satisfy.

## Corrections

Fix minor spelling or formatting issues directly. For material factual changes, correct the article promptly and add a dated note describing what changed and why. Never silently remove a central claim. If the premise is substantially wrong, add a prominent editor's note or retract while preserving a transparent record. Route reader reports through the contact form with reason “correction.”

## Pre-publication checklist

- [ ] Title, excerpt, category, slug, author, tags, and cover alt text are complete.
- [ ] Every factual claim, number, quotation, and link has been checked.
- [ ] The strongest reasonable counterargument or limitation is represented.
- [ ] Disclosures, conflicts, sponsorship, affiliates, and AI assistance are accurate.
- [ ] Financial, health, legal, or safety claims received appropriate review.
- [ ] Headings are descriptive and ordered; link text makes sense out of context.
- [ ] SEO title, description, canonical, and social preview are accurate.
- [ ] The preview was checked on mobile and desktop, including dark mode.
- [ ] Publication time and featured status are intentional.
- [ ] A second person reviewed material or high-risk work.
